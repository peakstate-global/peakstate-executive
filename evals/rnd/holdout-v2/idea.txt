Idea: have every driver and warehouse worker spend 15 minutes on a commercial brain-training app at the start of each shift, to sharpen attention and cut safety incidents.

Today our 2,100 drivers and warehouse staff start each shift with a five-minute toolbox talk. Under the idea, each person would also complete a 15-minute set of memory, attention and reaction-time games on a tablet before work starts, and supervisors would review weekly scores. The app licence would cost about $240,000 a year, and the extra paid time comes to about 2,600 hours a week across the business.

The case I have heard for it: a 2022 University of Oxford study found that drivers who did brain training before each shift had 31% fewer safety incidents over a year. The app vendor also sent us three case studies from transport customers, and the drivers who tried the trial version said the games made them feel sharper.

About us: an Australian road freight and logistics company with about 3,200 staff.
