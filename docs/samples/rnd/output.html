Research note for review.

Executive summary

The answer: Do not rebuild the programme as described; run the bounded internal test only if the organisation wants local proof, accepting one workshop’s design effort and participant time as the price.

Point 1: The broad evidence does not justify four matched versions of every workshop. The most recent direct meta-analysis found a small average benefit, but the required crossover interaction (each style doing best in its matched version) appeared in only 26% of measures. Strongest evidence: 21 studies, 101 effect sizes and 1,712 participants produced an overall effect of g = 0.31, yet only 26% of measures supported matching [3]. [frontiersin.org]

Point 2: Limited results show that matching can help under narrow conditions. One randomised study found improved comprehension for clear visual-preferring learners, but not equivalent benefit for its auditory-ambiguous group. Strongest evidence: the study tested 42 learners and concluded that its results only “partly support” matching [4]. [frontiersin.org]

Point 3: A one-month pilot can falsify (show that a claim does not survive a fair test) a large matching effect, but cannot validate a rebuild across 12 workshops and about 600 leaders. I read “test exactly as described” as creating all four versions of one existing workshop, not rebuilding all 12 during the test. Strongest evidence: the meta-analysis states that all included studies were single sessions and could not establish long-term effects [3]. [frontiersin.org]

This works if:

  • Every style has enough participants and each matched version wins on objective learning.
  • A later, larger test confirms the crossover pattern.
  • The benefit exceeds the opportunity cost (what else internal designers could have done).

It fails if:

  • Satisfaction rises but delayed retention does not.
  • One format works best for everyone.
  • Any style group is too small for a credible comparison.

One-month test

Who takes part: Recruit 64 volunteer people leaders, seeking 16 who self-identify with each style. Do not buy or administer a vendor profile. Stop before delivery if any style has fewer than 12 participants.

What they do: Rebuild one current workshop into visual, auditory, reading/writing and kinaesthetic versions. Within each self-identified style, randomly assign half to the matched version and half to one pre-specified unmatched version. Keep learning objectives, content, facilitator time and assessment identical. Do not tell participants whether they were matched.

What we measure: Use one pre-test, an immediate post-test, a scored leadership scenario and delayed retention (what remains four weeks after the workshop). Score the scenario without knowing allocation. Treat satisfaction, engagement and perceived fit as secondary outcomes. Record internal design hours and participant time.

Stop rule:

  • Stop if there is no crossover interaction across all four styles.
  • Stop if any unmatched group performs as well as or better than its matched group.
  • Stop if only satisfaction or engagement improves.
  • A positive result supports a larger confirmatory test, not programme-wide rebuilding.

Pitch

Recommendation: Keep the current programme architecture. Authorise only a no-vendor, one-workshop test if local evidence is important.

Why: The proposed model would multiply content maintenance, facilitation and measurement work. Public evidence does not show a dependable learning gain across all four styles. Preference and enthusiasm are not substitutes for retained knowledge or changed leadership behaviour.

What this buys: A controlled internal test can show whether a large matching effect exists in our own leadership context without committing vendor spend. It also creates an auditable measure of design effort and participant time.

Decision boundary: Do not fund profiles or rebuild the remaining workshops unless a larger confirmatory test reproduces objective gains for every style and those gains justify the added operating burden.

Where both sides are right

  • Claim 1: Matching can help when a learner has a clear preference and the task fits that modality. It does not justify four programme versions when equivalent benefits do not appear across the other styles.
  • Claim 2: The reported evidence could support the proposal when the study and result can be identified and quoted. It carries no evidential weight when neither can be verified.
  • Claim 3: Learner enthusiasm may improve participation when it is measured. It does not establish better learning when objective outcomes are unchanged.
  • Claim 4: Vendor cost matters when a current, comparable quotation exists. The note’s amount cannot enter the business case when it remains unverified.
  • Claim 5: Development cost matters when scope, quality standards and maintenance are specified. The note’s amount cannot enter the business case when it remains unverified.

Words used in this note

  • Crossover interaction: each learning-style group performs best with a different, corresponding version.
  • Delayed retention: what remains four weeks after the workshop.
  • Falsify: show that a claim does not survive a fair test.
  • Meshing hypothesis: the claim that matching instructional format to a preferred learning style improves learning.
  • Opportunity cost: what else internal designers could have done with the same time.
