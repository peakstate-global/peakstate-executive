Research note for review.

Executive summary

The answer: Do not rebuild the programme as described; take forward only a no-vendor-spend matched-versus-non-matched test with a pre-agreed decision gate.

Point 1: The direct evidence is mixed: a 2024 meta-analysis found a small pooled benefit, but valid crossover effects were infrequent and study quality was low. Strongest evidence: only 26% of outcomes showed the required crossover. Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1428732/full [frontiersin.org]

Point 2: Preference is not the same as aptitude: one reviewed experiment found only 44.6% agreement between self-classification and an objective questionnaire. Strongest evidence: weak self-identification. Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2023.1147498/full [frontiersin.org]

Point 3: Leadership-specific evidence supports personalising practice to a manager’s work context, goals and feedback needs, not routing people by sensory category. Strongest evidence: randomised manager training with behavioural practice and feedback. Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0320131

This works if: stated preferences remain stable, matched delivery improves delayed retention and transfer beyond non-matched delivery, every style benefits, and the gain justifies four-version operating effort.

It fails if: preferences change, one delivery method performs well across categories, enjoyment rises without transfer, or the test cannot distinguish treatment effects from topic or facilitator effects.

One-month test

Who takes part: 24 managers, six per self-identified VARK category; no paid profile.

What they do: Each completes two parallel 45-minute leadership lessons, one matched and one deliberately non-matched. Randomise topic, order and non-matched format; keep objectives, examples, duration, facilitator and assessment constant.

What we measure: Baseline, immediate and week-three knowledge; blind-rated application to a new leadership scenario; preference stability; completion and acceptability.

Stop rule: Do not progress to commercial design unless matched delivery wins on both delayed knowledge and transfer, preferences remain repeatable, and no category is materially worse.

Pitch

Recommendation: Test the mechanism, not the full proposition. The available evidence does not support rebuilding twelve workshops into four tracks before we know matching improves leadership outcomes.

Value: A counterbalanced internal pilot gives a clean answer without vendor spend. It separates learning-style matching from general personalisation, multimodal teaching and participant preference.

Measurement: Use delayed knowledge and blind-rated application, not satisfaction alone. Record internal design hours so the test shows what additional complexity buys.

Decision gate: Scale only if matched delivery produces a repeatable gain across styles that is large enough to justify four versions. Otherwise, direct investment towards contextual needs, practice, feedback and workplace application.

Integration

  • Claim 1: Conditional: Matching improves comprehension holds when a genuine crossover replicates under constant content and assessment; the broader negative evidence holds when one format dominates or the interaction disappears.
  • Claim 2: Reframe: Shared assumption: A measured learning dimension is a stable causal aptitude. Association with performance does not justify routing instruction by that dimension.
  • Claim 3: Reframe: Shared assumption: All forms of personalisation use the same mechanism. Personalisation by behavioural need, work context, goals and feedback can be retained without VARK categorisation.
  • Claim 4: Conditional: The proposed pilot discriminates matching effects holds when topic, order, exposure, facilitator and assessment are controlled; the adverse interpretation holds when it measures only enjoyment, immediate recall or unmatched topics of unequal difficulty.
  • Claim 5: Reframe: Shared assumption: The claimed Harvard study exists and matches the note. Exclude the claim from the executive case unless a traceable, accessible full-text study is produced.
  • Claim 6: Reframe: Shared assumption: Leaders can classify themselves consistently and will accept being routed by that classification. Measure classification stability and acceptability before treating either as an implementation condition.
  • Claim 7: Reframe: Shared assumption: The quoted commercial costs and internal four-version effort are known. Collect comparable written scopes and record internal design, facilitation, governance and maintenance hours before making an economic case.
