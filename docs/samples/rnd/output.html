Integration

  • Claim 1: Conditional: The supportive result holds when a clear visual preference is matched to visual text in a small immediate-comprehension study; the adverse judgement holds when repeatable crossover effects across styles, delayed retention and transfer are required.
  • Claim 2: Reframe: Shared assumption: personalisation requires fixed VARK categories and four separate versions. Dropping it allows personal relevance, choice and multiple modes without classifying each leader.
  • Claim 3: Reframe: Shared assumption: the claimed Harvard result is a dependable basis for investment. Dropping it means the decision must rest on the verifiable literature and a controlled internal test.
  • Claim 4: Reframe: Shared assumption: knowing or liking a preferred format predicts better learning. Dropping it means preference and satisfaction must be measured separately from retention and transfer.

One-month test

Reading: “Preferred learning style” is read as a leader’s self-reported primary preference among visual, auditory, reading/writing and kinaesthetic. A “matched version” means identical learning objectives, facts, examples, duration and assessment, with only the principal delivery mode changed. This is the most reasonable reading because the note proposes profiling each leader and matching one of four versions.

Purpose: Test the narrow causal proposition before testing a programme redesign: does matched delivery improve delayed retention or leadership-scenario transfer compared with non-matched delivery? The test would not assess a vendor profile, annual programme economics or the value of personalisation generally.

Participants: Use 24–32 volunteer people leaders from the organisation, drawn from more than one business area. Aim for at least six in each self-selected category. If one category has too few volunteers, report that as a feasibility finding rather than reclassifying people. Use only internal learning staff and facilitators.

Profile: Give participants neutral, plain-language descriptions of the four categories and require one primary selection for test purposes. Do not buy, copy or imitate a proprietary questionnaire. Record confidence in the selection, because an uncertain category weakens any claim about matching.

Content: Select two short, comparable segments from existing leadership material. Keep the learning objectives, factual content, examples, practice time and assessment unchanged. Produce only the minimum experimental variants: a diagram-led visual format; a spoken audio format; a text-and-written-reflection format; and a hands-on scenario or card-sort format.

Procedure: Each participant completes both segments. One is delivered in the participant’s selected format and the other in a randomly selected non-matched format. Counterbalance topic, order and matched condition so that each topic appears equally often as matched and non-matched. Facilitators should not tell participants which condition is being treated as matched.

Measures: Before each segment, use a short prior-knowledge check. Immediately afterwards, measure common-content recall and confidence. On day 21, use the same delayed-retention test and a new leadership scenario requiring application of the material. Also record completion time, satisfaction, perceived fit, facilitator time and internal development hours.

Analysis: Make delayed retention the primary outcome and scenario transfer the second outcome. Compare each participant’s matched and non-matched results, adjusted for prior knowledge. Separately inspect whether the best delivery format differs by stated category; that crossover is the critical test. Keep satisfaction separate so that a format cannot be judged effective merely because it was liked.

Stop rule: Stop the four-version rebuilding idea if matched delivery produces no positive matched-minus-unmatched difference on delayed retention and no style-by-format crossover on scenario transfer. Do not proceed to vendor procurement or a full programme rebuild on satisfaction alone.

Limits: This is a falsification pilot, not a definitive effectiveness or return-on-investment study. A positive signal would justify considering a larger preregistered internal trial. A null result would answer the immediate question without vendor spend.

Pitch

Evidence: The direct evidence for rebuilding leadership development around fixed VARK preferences is weak. One small experiment found a context-specific comprehension benefit, but the leading review, recent meta-analysis and accessible randomised trial do not support broad implementation. The strongest claimed result in the note could not be verified.

Budget: The programme would require four maintained versions of every workshop plus a profiling process. The cost figures in the note are unverified, so they should not anchor an investment decision. The maintenance, assurance and facilitator burden would still increase because each workshop would have four controlled variants.

Question: The useful decision question is narrower than “should we personalise leadership development?” It is whether matching the delivery mode to a stated VARK preference improves delayed retention or transfer enough to justify further work.

Proposal: Run a one-month internal experiment with 24–32 people leaders, two short pieces of existing content and no vendor. Change only the delivery mode required to create matched and non-matched conditions. Use common assessments and counterbalanced allocation.

Value: The test would produce four practical outputs: delayed-retention results, leadership-scenario transfer results, learner reaction data and the internal effort required to create and maintain variants. That separates learning value from preference and gives the learning team evidence about what the model would buy.

Guardrails: Use our own people, facilitators and content. Do not procure a profile, commission workshop versions or make claims about permanent learner types. Do not treat enjoyment as proof of learning.

Decision gate: If matching does not improve delayed retention and does not produce the required crossover in transfer, stop the rebuilding idea. If a clear directional signal appears, the next choice would be whether to run a properly powered trial before developing an investment case. The one-month test informs that choice; it does not make it.
